## Problem

✍️ **Written, ~95 words** 🟢
> Architects spend most of a project drafting rather than designing. Turning a design into
> a buildable model still means placing every wall, duct and pipe by hand, and checking
> each one against the building code as you go. We have spoken with 78 architects about
> this, and most of them described weeks of drafting and rework that leave less time for
> the design itself.

(The solution half is below. Join them when a form asks "problem and solution" together.)

🎤 **Spoken, ~60 words** 🟡
> A software engineer writes one line and AI finishes the function. An architect draws one
> wall, then the next, for two months. Sixty-eight percent of that time is compliance, not
> design. Two thousand hours a project, chasing code. We spoke to 78 architects to check we
> weren't imagining it.

🎤 **Spoken, with the personal open** 🟢 story / 🟡 statistics
> An architect almost made my dad go bankrupt. It was 2024. He runs a construction company
> and he took me to the biggest client pitch of his life. Three days out, the architect
> said the first draft wouldn't be ready. We walked in empty-handed.

🔴 *68% and 2,000 hours come from the deck. Find the source before using them anywhere
that will be diligenced. "$240,000 lost per project" (2 Sep LinkedIn draft) has no source
in this vault. Don't use it until it does.*

## Solution

✍️ **Written, ~50 words** 🟢
> Civly takes an architect's design and drafts the model for them, with the structure and
> mechanical services routed to match what they intended. The architect can then ask for
> changes in plain English, and nothing goes anywhere until they have reviewed it.

✍️ **Written, longer, ~85 words** 🟢
> Civly takes a CAD file, a sketch or a Rhino model and drafts the building model from it,
> with the structure and mechanical services routed to match the architect's design. From
> there the architect can make changes by describing them in plain English, such as making
> every window on a floor the same, or check an existing design against the building code
> instead of reading the code line by line. Every change is reviewed and approved by a
> person before it is kept.

🟡 *"LOD 250" is used in the pitch. It holds for architecture and structure. Don't attach
it to MEP without saying schematic.*

## Product status, at three honesty levels

✍️ **For a general form**
> Civly runs today on top of Revit. It can read, create, change and document a model, and
> it includes a set of mechanical services tools that no other public Revit integration
> offers. What it builds is ordinary, editable Revit geometry that an engineer can move,
> undo and check.

✍️ **For a technical reviewer**
> Civly is an MCP server plus a C# Revit plugin, built on the open-source
> mcp-servers-for-revit project, which started with 29 tools and no mechanical services
> commands. We are now at 62. In real Revit sessions it has placed and connected 38 of 38
> ducts, 19 of 19 fittings and 19 of 19 terminals, and removed 819 elements in a single
> undoable step. Our duct sizing matches Revit's own calculations to rounding. Every change
> runs as a named transaction with a dry run and an approval step.

✍️ **What we don't claim** 🟢 (for any form that asks about limitations)
> Testing in live Revit covers about 9% of the tool set so far. Reading models, the safety
> checks and undo are proven there. Most creation tools have been proven against a test
> harness but not yet in a live model. Only Revit 2026 is verified. We lay out schematic
> supply ductwork; we don't design HVAC systems.

## Business model and pricing

✍️ **Written** 🟡
> We plan to sell a studio licence at $2,000 per seat per month. That is more than typical
> software, but one seat is designed to do the drafting work of four to five people. On a
> typical $5 million project, drafting labour runs to about $120,000, or roughly $20,000 a
> month over six months, so the licence costs about a tenth of the work it replaces. Our
> goal before raising a seed round is five paid pilots and $10,000 in monthly revenue.

🔴 *The $120k figure is a founder estimate, not sourced.* If asked "why not $30 a seat",
lead with the replacement ratio, never the absolute number (Sasha, 5 Aug).

## Moat / defensibility

✍️ **Written** 🟢
> The code on its own isn't the moat, since anyone can build on a modelling API. What is
> hard to copy is the data: matched pairs of an architect's design and the finished,
> coordinated model an engineer actually produced. We are building those through data
> partnerships with firms, and using them to teach Civly where things should go, not just
> how to place them.
>
> We learned something early that shapes how we do this. Autodesk's own reference model
> contains real engineering errors, including one duct running at 66 times its target
> friction. A system trained only to copy data like that would learn the mistakes, so we
> calculate everything that physics decides and only learn the choices that come down to
> practice.

🟢 The Snowdon finding is verified and it's the strongest technical answer in this bank.
Use it whenever a form asks about defensibility or technical depth.

## Do not say

The full list is in [[Civly Architecture Reference]]. These are the ones that catch
applications most often:

- ❌ "secure" · ❌ "your data never leaves your network"
- ❌ "works in Revit" without a result file · ❌ "supports Revit 2020 to 2027" (2026 only)
- ❌ "designs HVAC" (it lays out schematic supply)
- ❌ "no public Revit MCP has MEP commands" (falsified 4 Aug). Say: **no public Revit MCP
  ships a dedicated, documented MEP creation tier**
- ❌ "hundreds of architects" → **78**
- ❌ "Four months in." as a standalone sentence in a written form
- ❌ "I sold my last company" with no story attached
- ❌ anything about AI replacing jobs
