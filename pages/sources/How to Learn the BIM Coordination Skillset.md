## TL;DR

Comprehensive guide to the BIM coordinator role: what programs to learn, real-life examples of coordination tasks, and how to transition from Revit modeller to BIM coordinator. Covers services coordination, plant room access, louvre/facade coordination, precast coordination — all grounded in real project examples. Strong market signal: architects and engineers spend 5–7 years as modellers before making this transition, and most feel stagnant because they don't learn coordination early.

## Key Claims / Techniques

- **BIM coordination programs stack:** Revit (modelling) → Navisworks (clash detection) → additional discipline tools
- **Coordination types covered:**
  - Services coordination (MEP runs vs. structural elements)
  - Plant room access coordination (equipment fit, maintenance clearances)
  - Louvre/facade coordination (MEP penetrations through building envelope)
  - Precast coordination (precast panels vs. MEP penetrations)
- **Career path:** Revit modeller (0–6 months) → BIM coordinator → BIM manager → project manager. The coordination step is what unlocks management roles.
- **Market reality:** Most architects/engineers stay as modellers for 5–7 years due to inertia. Those who learn coordination early advance rapidly.
- **Three levels of growth:** (1) Level up skills → (2) opportunity presents → (3) take opportunity = career advancement
- **Coordinator vs. modeller distinction:** Coordinators make key decisions; modellers receive information and execute. Civly's output needs to empower coordinators, not just produce models.
