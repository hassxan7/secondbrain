## TL;DR
Reuben identifies **competition-bid production** as a credible architecture wedge: competition brief/sketch → concept plans, 3D, renders, and submission panels, sold first to boutique practices that currently burn unbillable labour pursuing work. At the same time, after seeing Civly's actual demo, he says it is an **engineering-consultant product rather than an architectural-design product**, independently reinforcing Hesh's MEP-drafting direction. His main instruction is to choose one user, workflow, output, and sales cycle rather than combining competition design, engineering drafting, late model edits, and coordination.

## Key Claims / Techniques
- Architecture work often begins with a competition brief. Reuben estimates roughly 80% of projects are pursued through some form of competitive bid, including invited competitions.
- Competition teams iterate crude sketches quickly, then produce concept plans, a 3D model, renders, narrative, and prescribed PDF panels. Detailed MEP and construction-grade BIM are not required.
- The competition wedge converts **unbillable speculative labour** into more submissions and a higher chance of winning work.
- Best initial ICP: boutique/mid-sized architecture firms; enterprise adoption is slow and their pain is weaker.
- BIM draftspersons enter after plans are substantially settled. Their work is generally billable and embedded in Revit libraries, standards, and consultant workflows, making generic architecture drafting automation harder to sell.
- Reuben's response to Civly's demo: its structure/compliance/engineering decisions fit fire, electrical, ESD, structural, and MEP consultants better than architects.
- Hesh's MEP schematic → BIM proposal is therefore a separate, valid consultancy workflow—not the same product as the competition-bid generator.
- A competition product needs a new demo and output: brief/sketch → floor plans + differentiated 3D design + renders + submission-ready PDF panels.
- Revit replacement is the wrong battle. Civly must either operate before Revit matters or remain an assistive/interoperable layer.
- Scope rule: **one target market, one client, one sales cycle.**

## Civly Relevance
**Score: HIGH.** The source resolves why Civly keeps oscillating between architecture and engineering: both opportunities are real, but they occur in different project phases and require different products. It also shows that the **current build**—deterministic compliance/calcs, IFC output, MEP routing, and human approval—fits the MEP/engineering-consultant wedge more closely. The competition wedge is worth validating commercially, but building it now would be a pivot into creative generation, rendering, and document composition.
