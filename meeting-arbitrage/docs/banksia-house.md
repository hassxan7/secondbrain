# What to show the house

Ten minutes at a meeting. Do it in this order — the order is doing work, because
the money question is the one that sinks it if it comes first.

## 1. Open the board (2 min)

Put the link on the TV or pass a phone around. Say one sentence:

> "It's the whiteboard, on your phone, and it counts."

Let them find their own column. Somebody will tick something. That is the demo.

## 2. The rule (1 min)

There is one, and it fits on a line:

> **4 tasks a week, or it costs you $100.**
> Everyone puts $100 in at the start of term. Do 4 tasks and you get it back.

Say the second half properly, because it is the part that makes it work: the
money is collected **up front**. Nobody gets invoiced, nobody has to chase
anyone, and nobody has to be the person who brings it up at dinner. At the end
of term you either get your $100 back or you don't.

That is the entire change from the current system, which has the same rule and
no way to enforce it.

## 3. What it will send you (2 min)

Read this out. It is the part people are actually worried about.

- At most **three messages a week**. Ever.
- Nothing between **9pm and 8am**.
- **Never** about something you have already done.
- One nudge Thursday if you are short. One Sunday if you are still short.
  There is no third one.

If somebody is still uneasy, the cap is in the code, in one file, and they can
read it.

## 4. The anonymous bar (2 min)

Show the Raise screen.

> "Pick the area, not the person."

Explain the two rules that make it not a weapon:

- One person raising something **waits 24 hours** before the house sees it, so
  it can't be a same-day jab at whoever you just argued with.
- Two people raising the same area goes up **immediately**, as a house issue —
  "two housemates flagged the kitchen", never a name.

Names typed into the note are stripped out before it is stored. The system
knows who raised it; the house never does.

## 5. "Who did this?" (2 min)

Show the pan-of-noodles example.

> "Everyone gets asked once. Was me, or not me."
>
> "Say 'was me' and it's closed, immediately, nothing happens. Say nothing and
> you're listed as having said nothing."

Be straight about why: if only the people who reply are counted, ignoring the
question is the winning move, and everyone works that out inside a month.

## 6. Joining (1 min)

Send them the link. Four questions, about a minute:

1. Your name
2. A mobile **or** an email — one is enough
3. When you're free each week — with **"I may not be free every week"** and
   **"sync my calendar"** as options
4. What the data is used for, in full, before you hand anything over

## The three questions you will get

**"Who's holding the $100?"**
Somebody has to. The app tracks who is clear; it does not hold money. Pick a
person, or hold it in a shared account. If the house is not comfortable with
that, run a term with the tracking on and no money at all and see if the board
alone changes anything — the numbers will tell you.

**"What if I tick something I didn't do?"**
Anyone can challenge a tick, which suspends it until the meeting. But the real
answer is that this is a system for a house that is mostly honest and
occasionally forgetful. It is not an audit.

**"What if I can't make the meeting?"**
Declaring a recurring commitment — Isaac's Monday football — is free and
permanent, and every clashing slot is ruled out forever. It costs you nothing.
What costs you is skipping a meeting you said you could make: that reduces how
much your availability weighs on picking the next one. Showing up buys influence
over the time. It does not work the other way around.

## The one thing to say at the end

> "Nothing here needs anyone to be the bad guy. That's the whole point."
