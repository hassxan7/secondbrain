> [!tip] Civly Relevance — **HIGH**
> Reuben maps the architecture workflow and exposes a second credible wedge: helping boutique practices produce competition bids faster, before coordination and detailed BIM begin. He also confirms that Civly's current engineering/compliance demo is better suited to individual engineering consultants than architects, which independently supports Hesh's MEP-consultancy direction. The call is strategically important because these are two different users, workflows, outputs, and sales motions—not one broad product.

## Notes

### Reuben's framing of Civly's original pitch
- Civly presented an ambition to take a CAD/sketch input to approximately LOD 250, using paired input/output data to train an ML model.
- Reuben immediately asked what “LOD 250” meant and where in the real project timeline Civly intended to enter.
- His central warning: a product spanning sketch-to-model creation, late model editing, engineering compliance, and BIM coordination is addressing several separate workflows and target markets at once.
- Revit is the incumbent collaboration environment for major projects. Civly should not try to become a replacement Revit; it must either enter before Revit matters or act as an assistive layer that preserves the existing workflow.

### Two ways architecture firms obtain work
- A practice may receive a repeat/direct commission, but Reuben says a large share of architecture work—he estimated roughly 80%—is pursued through competition or bid briefs.
- In a competition, the organiser supplies the brief and submission requirements.
- A principal architect or design lead rapidly sketches options; a small team iterates plans and massing over roughly two to four weeks, often working long hours.
- Early iterations are intentionally crude. The team should not build a fully resolved BIM while the concept is still changing.
- Once a plan is about 80% settled, the team creates the 3D model, renders, floor plans, narrative, and required PDF panels.
- The competition output is usually presentation material—PDF panels, plans, renders, and text—not a construction-ready BIM or coordinated MEP model.
- If the practice wins, the competition material becomes a reference. The design is rebuilt/refined for development approval and design development.

### Wedge 1 — competition-bid production
- Reuben's strongest recommendation is to automate the unbillable, high-risk competition stage.
- The ideal product lets one strong principal/designer:
  - ingest the competition brief;
  - iterate from sketches or scaled plans;
  - generate coordinated floor plans and a 3D concept;
  - create multiple visualisations/renders;
  - assemble the exact required panel sizes and final PDF submission.
- The value proposition is not merely “draft faster.” It is:
  - submit more competitions with the same team;
  - reduce speculative labour and all-nighters;
  - increase the chance of winning fee-generating projects;
  - reduce a competition team from several people toward one principal plus limited presentation support.
- Boutique and mid-sized practices have the sharpest pain because they cannot afford to burn the same speculative labour as large firms.
- Enterprise firms may adopt later after smaller firms prove an advantage. Selling into a large firm's technology/testing group could take years.
- Reuben suggests project/competition-based pricing: first competition free or low-risk, then pay per new bid.
- The product has lower technical and liability risk here because the output is competition-ready presentation material, not construction documentation.
- Differentiation concern: architects will ask whether all AI-assisted designs look alike. Civly must demonstrate different designers producing clearly different outputs and frame itself as a tool whose result depends on the user's design judgement.

### Why this is not the same as BIM drafting
- Reuben separates early competition design from post-win BIM production.
- BIM/architectural draftspersons are typically engaged once floor plans are substantially resolved and there is a defined model to build.
- At that later stage, modelling time is often billable to the client. Speed can reduce billable hours rather than create obvious new revenue.
- Small residential practices may have only one drafter and thin margins, weakening the “replace three or four drafters” pitch.
- Large practices have existing Revit libraries, supplier families, standards, trained staff, and coordination expectations. A new drafting environment has a substantial ecosystem disadvantage.
- Even an assistive Revit copilot must overcome implementation risk: firms fear corrupting a model developed over months or receiving non-compliant output that requires costly rework.

### Reuben's response to Hesh's MEP-drafting thesis
- Hassaan and Yash described Hesh's proposal: take MEP schematics and automate the work of several BIM drafters.
- Reuben did not reject the MEP consultancy use case. He located it after the architectural design is settled and warned that its economics and user are different from competition work.
- After watching Civly's demo, he said it looked like an **engineering product**, not an architectural-design product:
  - the demo emphasised structure, compliance, engineering decisions, and coordinated discipline output;
  - architects would object that their designs are fluid and stylistically distinctive;
  - fire, electrical, ESD, structural, and MEP consultants work with logical constraints and receive an architectural model as an input.
- His practical conclusion was that the existing demo should be sold to individual engineering consultants if Civly continues that path.
- A competition product would require a completely different demo centred on brief-to-panels creative iteration.

### Architecture project sequence after a competition win
- The winning concept is refined into floor plans and taken through development approval.
- During design development, the client engages engineering consultants; the architect remains the primary consultant.
- The architectural model is shared with fire, electrical, ESD, structural, and MEP specialists.
- Each consultant overlays or links a discipline model and contributes its constrained system.
- Coordination meetings and a BIM manager arrive only at this later stage; they manage shared coordinates, model issues, and consultant clashes.
- This confirms that competition design, discipline drafting, and BIM coordination are separate phases.

### Go-to-market advice
- Pick one target market, one client, and one sales cycle.
- For competition bids:
  - target boutique/mid-sized architectural firms;
  - quantify spend on the last competition and compare it with Civly-assisted delivery;
  - promise more bids and lower speculative cost, not headcount cuts;
  - obtain permission to use successful firms and wins in marketing;
  - demonstrate at BIM/architecture events and directly to principals.
- For the current engineering demo:
  - approach individual engineering consultants rather than architectural designers;
  - be specific about the exact task removed from their workflow;
  - preserve Revit interoperability and existing firm libraries/standards.
- Enterprise architecture practices have slow adoption cycles and low urgency; they are not the best first design partners for a disruptive workflow.

### Product boundaries surfaced by the call
- **Competition product:** competition brief/sketch → concept plans, 3D, renders, submission panels/PDF.
- **Engineering drafting product:** architect's settled model or engineer's schematic → discipline-specific model/output.
- **Late BIM copilot:** edits to an established Revit model using firm libraries and context.
- **Coordination product:** clashes, assignments, issue management across consultant models.
- These products can share geometry/agent infrastructure later, but they cannot share one initial promise, demo, buyer, or validation test.

## Civly Relevance
**Score: HIGH**

Reuben does not invalidate Hesh. He identifies a second opportunity and clarifies that the current Civly build already aligns more naturally with Hesh's engineering-consultant wedge. His competition-bid recommendation is commercially attractive but requires a new input/output target, a creative-generation and presentation pipeline, a different buyer, and a different demo. For Civly's current architecture, the call is strongest as a scope-control warning: do not combine brief-to-panels, schematic-to-MEP IFC, late model editing, and clash coordination into one MVP.
