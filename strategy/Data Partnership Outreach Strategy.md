The 30-day playbook for landing **data partnerships with MEP consultancies** (and BIM-drafting firms) and, in the same conversations, getting answers to our **advisor/spec questions**. Owner: **Hassaan** (BD); Yash joins for technical validation. Builds on [[Data Partnership Moat]] and [[Advisor List & Question Routing]].

## Goal (next 30 days)
1. **Data partnerships:** sign a handful of MEP consultancies into a "give us schematic→model pairs, get early/free access" arrangement under NDA. Target: **5+ firms agreeing in principle**, pipeline of ~50–100 contacted.
2. **Spec/advisor answers:** use the same calls to answer the [[Technical Roadmap — What to Build First|Phase 0 blocker]] — the exact input file → output file → process — and the 5 research questions.

> [!note] Two different funnels
> Civly's customer GTM is **warm-intro only** (Michael/Leo's networks — see [[Civly - Traction and Funding]]). **Data partnerships are a separate, volume motion** — direct outreach to MEP consultancies. Don't confuse the two; the data ask is lower-stakes for them (early access for data), so cold/warm outreach is appropriate here.

## The core insight: lead with learning, not "give us your data"
A cold "send us your Revit models" gets ignored. A warm "we're building AI for MEP drafting and would love 15 minutes of your expertise" gets replies — and **opens the data door naturally** once rapport exists. So the first touch is a **learning/expert ask**, not a data ask. This also doubles as our advisor-question pipeline.

**The ask ladder:**
```
1. Connect (LinkedIn) / short email  → no ask but a compliment + one line on what we do
2. 15-min "learn from you" call       → answer our spec + advisor questions; show value
3. Show the demo / value               → "here's what we're building"
4. Float the partnership               → early/free access in exchange for training data
5. NDA + first data drop               → training-only, never shared
```

## Who to target
- **Primary:** MEP consultancies in Australia, **Sydney first** (easy to meet in person), then nationwide.
- **Secondary:** BIM-drafting / architectural-draftsperson firms, mechanical subcontractors with in-house drafting (the LOD 0→250 cohort).
- **Roles to reach, in order:** BIM Manager / BIM Coordinator → MEP/Mechanical Engineer → Director/Principal (small firms) → Drafters (warm, candid, but not decision-makers).
- **Sourcing:** LinkedIn (use Yash's Premium trial, expires 29 Jun), Google "MEP consultancy Sydney/Melbourne", industry directories, Hesh's and Dhanjeet's networks for warm intros.

## Channel strategy (and why)
| Channel | Use for | Why | Realistic volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** (primary) | First touch at scale; finding the right person | Searchable by role/firm; profile does the credibility work; low friction | 15–20 connects/day |
| **Email** (secondary) | When you have the address; slightly more formal firms | Async, lets them forward internally | 5–10/day |
| **Phone** (highest conversion) | Warm leads, small firms, anyone who half-replied | Hesh's "call 500" — fastest to a yes/no, builds real rapport | 3–5 calls/day |

**Sequence:** LinkedIn connect → (on accept) message → if engaged, move to a call/meeting → partnership ask. Phone for anyone warm or local. Don't pitch in the connection note; earn the reply first.

## The copy (use verbatim; no em dashes, say IFC not Revit, don't oversell)

### A. LinkedIn connection note (<300 chars, no ask)
> Hi [Name], I'm building Civly, an AI tool that turns an MEP engineer's schematic into a first-draft BIM model. You've spent years in [firm/discipline] and I'd genuinely value learning how drafting really works on the ground. Would love to connect.

### B. LinkedIn first message (after they accept)
> Thanks for connecting [Name]. Quick context: my co-founder and I are building Civly, an AI BIM drafter. We take a design or an MEP schematic and produce a first-draft, code-compliant model, so a small team can do the drafting of a much bigger one.
>
> We're not from the industry, so we're talking to experienced people like you to get it right. Could I borrow 15 minutes to hear how your team takes a schematic to a model today, and where the time actually goes? Happy to work around your schedule.

### C. Cold email
> **Subject:** 15 minutes on how MEP drafting really works?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm Hassaan, co-founder of Civly. We're building an AI tool that turns an MEP engineer's schematic into a first-draft, code-compliant BIM model, so a firm can produce a draft in a fraction of the usual time.
>
> We're early and we want to build it around how the work is actually done, not how we imagine it. You've done this for years at [firm], so I'd value 15 minutes to learn how your team goes from a schematic to a model, and where the real bottlenecks are.
>
> If it's useful, we're also partnering with a small number of consultancies to shape the product, with early access in return. No pressure, mainly I'd love to learn.
>
> Would a short call next week work?
>
> Hassaan
> Civly | [link]

### D. Phone / call opener (script)
> "Hi [Name], this is Hassaan from Civly. I'll be quick. We're building AI that turns an MEP schematic into a first-draft BIM model, and rather than guess how the work is done, we're calling people who actually do it. Could I ask you a couple of questions about how your team drafts, and share what we're building? Five minutes, and if it's not useful you can hang up on me."

### E. In-call flow (the 15 minutes)
1. **Listen first (8 min):** How do you go from a schematic to a model? What format is the input (which CAD type)? What eats the most time? Where do errors creep in? Ask the [[Advisor List & Question Routing|5 research questions]] + the input/output spec. "Tell me about the last time" when answers go vague.
2. **Show value (3 min):** "Here's what we're building" — the demo / the feasibility tool. Frame it as *making their team faster*, never *replacing their people*.
3. **Float the partnership (4 min):** "We're training on real schematic-to-model pairs from a few consultancies. In exchange you get early and free access while we build. Everything is under NDA, used only to train our model, and never shared with anyone else. Would that be worth exploring?"

### F. The data-partnership ask + NDA framing (the bigger ask)
> What we're asking for: a set of your past projects as input CAD/schematic plus the output model, so our AI can learn how real drafting is done.
>
> What you get: early and free access to Civly for [X months], a say in what we build, and a faster drafting workflow.
>
> How we protect you: a signed NDA. Your data is used only to train our model. It is never shared, never resold, and your families and templates never leave. We can scope it to a few anonymised projects to start.

## Objection handling
| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We can't share client CAD / it's confidential." | "Totally understand. We'll sign your NDA, scope it to a few anonymised projects, and it's training-only, never shared. You already hand models to subcontractors. This is the same trust, with paperwork." |
| "What's in it for us?" | "Early and free access while we build, a faster drafting workflow, and direct input into a tool built for your job. You're shaping it, not buying it." |
| "We're too busy." | "That's exactly the problem we're solving. Even 15 minutes helps, and the data ask can be a single old project whenever you have a moment." |
| "Won't this replace our drafters?" | "It makes a small team do more, the way Cursor did for developers. An engineer still owns and signs off every decision. We're giving your people leverage, not removing them." |
| "Does it actually work?" | "It's early and improving fast. That's why we want real projects, to make it accurate before anyone relies on it. We're upfront about where it is today." |

## Tracking (one simple sheet)
Columns: `Firm | Contact | Role | Channel | Date contacted | Stage (connected/replied/call booked/call done/partnership/NDA/data received) | Notes | Next action`. Review weekly. Watch the funnel ratios (connect→reply→call→partnership) to see which channel and message convert.

## 30-day plan
- **Week 1:** Build the target list (50–100 firms). Redeem LinkedIn Premium. Send the first 20 connects/day. Send the Hesh follow-up + ask him and Dhanjeet for 1–2 warm intros. Book the first 3 calls.
- **Week 2:** Run 5+ learning calls (answer the spec + advisor questions from these). Start emailing firms you can't reach on LinkedIn. Refine the message based on reply rates.
- **Week 3:** Float the partnership in warm calls. Send the NDA pack to the first 2–3 interested firms. Keep the top of funnel full (20 connects/day).
- **Week 4:** Close the first signed NDAs + first data drops. Review funnel metrics, double down on the best channel, write up what we learned (feeds [[Technical Roadmap — What to Build First|Phase 0/1]]).
